# Why couldn't these users find the command?

In GNOME's 2024 usability test of Files, P1 was asked to undo a move. Undo was there, in the Main Menu, and P1 gave up. The report adds one detail: none of the people who gave up on Undo opened the Main Menu. The command was available, but not where they looked.

One give-up could be one person's bad afternoon, so look at the same task across all six participants. On Undo, three could not complete it, two had very little difficulty and one had none. The other first give-ups split the group in a similar way: changing the icon size, Select All. The report grades each completed attempt by its difficulty and records separately who could not complete a task; an approach the study excluded counts as neither.

If a few weaker users caused the trouble, the same names would keep failing. Lay all eleven tasks against all six people and count the give-ups: eleven, spread over five tasks, and every participant gave up at least once. P1 found Select All with no difficulty and gave up on Undo; P5 had no difficulty with Undo and gave up on Select All. Who stalled depended on which command was asked for.

All eleven give-ups were on menu tasks. That alone proves little, because nine of the eleven tasks were menu tasks. The stronger evidence is the report's account of why people quit. Those who quit Undo did not open the Main Menu. Those who quit Select All reached for Ctrl+A or dragging instead. Those who quit Open Item Location had not checked the file's right-click menu. For three of these commands, the people who gave up did not look in the menu that held it; for the fourth, the icon size buttons in View Options, the report says the buttons were "very hard to notice".

Followed one at a time through the session, a participant's give-ups sit among easy tasks: P1 gave up on Undo and then sorted the files and switched to list view with no difficulty. The scores from 5 down to 1 on those journeys are this page's way to plot the report's colours; their spacing is not a measured interval.

These are six first-time GNOME users working with two test builds of Files 47. They point at specific places to investigate. They do not give a failure rate for all users, say how long a task took, or show that a later version behaves the same way. Within those limits the lesson is plain: putting a command in a menu made it available, but these users still had to think of opening that menu and then notice the command inside it.
