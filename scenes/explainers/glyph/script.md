# Why does the same text take different space?

“Hello, outlines” starts at the same left edge in Inter and in JetBrains Mono, at the same size. Yet the rulers end at 6.02 and 8.10 units. Which characters account for the extra 2.08?

Each character moves the pen on by an amount called its advance, and the next character starts where the pen stopped. A space moves the pen without drawing anything: it has an advance but no ink. The line's width is the sum of those movements, and the font decides each one. JetBrains Mono gives all fifteen characters 0.540, so its line is 15 × 0.540 = 8.10. Inter gives each l and i just 0.218, so the three l's and the i together gain 1.29 when the phrase moves to JetBrains Mono. The comma, the space and the t add 0.81 more. The H is the one letter Inter draws wider than JetBrains Mono's cell, and it gives 0.13 back. The words are the same; the font's spacing is not.

The ink is the other part of each glyph: an outline, the closed path the font draws. JetBrains Mono gives its l more than twice Inter's room, and it fills that room with a different outline: a flag at the top and a foot at the bottom, where Inter has a plain stem. Would a shape matcher still pair the two l's? Rayner's matcher compares outlines after removing position and size. Every glyph pairs with its own copy at 60%, all fourteen of them, since the space has no outline; but in this phrase none pairs with its counterpart in the other font. The same character does not mean the same outline: a font sets both how far each glyph moves the pen and what it draws.
