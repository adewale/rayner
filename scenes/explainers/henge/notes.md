# Construction and scope

The street setting changes the bearing from Manhattan's 299.1° to due west. The observer stays at 40.75° N and the sky stays unchanged. The tilt view shows two Earths from one viewpoint beside the orbit, so the axis keeps one direction on screen; it is dashed inside the translucent body. Each cuts New York's daily circle at the model's sunset hour angle for that solstice, so its sunlit share is the share of the Sun's day circle above the horizon.

The sky view draws New York's sky as a sphere with `seenFrom`, seen from outside and west of it (orthographic). Azimuth and altitude are longitude and latitude on it. Each day's path is the model's `sky()` swept through the hour angle, a small circle about the celestial pole, and ends at the model's own sunset hour angle; the street is the vertical great circle at its bearing. The year view is the same dome turned by one `Follow` over orbital longitude, from the December solstice round to the next, redrawing the day's circle from its declination at every frame; the gold band is the stretch of horizon the sunsets have covered. The annual chart then shows the same horizon crossing against the calendar. The street-level view is the sphere seen from its centre (gnomonic): great circles, the horizon and the street's vertical circle, are straight lines, and the schematic buildings are drawn through the same pinhole. One `Follow` lowers the gaze along the street's circle from 78° to 3° while the lens narrows, ending on the half-Sun evening of the first season. Street-level disc views then add the vertical information a top-down bearing cannot supply. The overhead view draws a schematic grid turned to the street's bearing, not Manhattan's real blocks; its sunrise direction is the same day's path reached at the opposite hour angle, and the winter mornings when the eastern end faces sunrise come from the same crossing calculation as the sunset alignments.

Seen from outside, the sphere keeps the sky's handedness, so from the west north is on the left; `seenFrom`'s view from the centre draws the sphere as a map, so for the street view the model mirrors north and south and a camera facing west has north on its right, as the picture's labels say. The dome and the street are separate steps rather than one move: a perspective eye must stay outside the sphere and `between` joins only viewpoints of one kind, so the camera move is the gnomonic tilt from the sky to the street. Every frame of both moves is held to `depthOrder`, and from the centre the street's circle and the horizon are held straight. The ground under the dome and Earth's night side are drawn as eight sectors each, since one face as large as a hemisphere is cut by the limb at two nearly opposite points and drawn off it.

The model uses a fixed 23.44° tilt, a circular orbit and spherical horizon coordinates. Longitude zero is the March equinox; the seasonal spacing is conceptual rather than a prediction of calendar dates. The annual sweep times the Sun's centre at a flat geometric horizon. Half/full comparisons use that same bearing at centre heights zero and one solar radius (0.2666°). The day gap between them converts orbital longitude at the mean rate, 365.2422 days per 360°; the real orbit moves more slowly in early July, so the gap is approximate. Refraction, terrain, observer elevation, local obstructions and the Sun's changing apparent size are omitted. The street views enlarge the disc and simplify the buildings. Actual AMNH event times require a specified visible horizon and an ephemeris; this page does not supply visiting times.

The native coordinate projection is checked against 48 vectors captured independently with ERFA's `hd2ae`, using pyerfa 2.0.1.5. `tools/oracle/henge.py` regenerates those vectors without importing Rayner's model. USNO's altitude/azimuth formula supplies the independent spherical identity; equinoxes, solstice extremes, constant sky under street rotation and the ordering of disc-height crossings are also checked.
