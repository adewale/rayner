# What does a WCAG conformance level require?

Press Tab on a payment form and focus moves from Cancel to Pay. If nothing on screen changes, you cannot tell which button Enter will press. An outline around Pay removes the guess. WCAG's success criterion 2.4.7, Focus Visible, asks for that outline, and WCAG files it at level AA.

So must a site show its focus? That depends on the conformance level its policy names. A team aiming for AA might expect to check the criteria labelled AA, and would miss most of the list: in WCAG 2.2, level AA asks for 55 criteria, the 31 at level A plus 24 at AA. Each level includes the levels below it, so level AAA asks for all 86.

The version matters as much as the level. Level AA asked for 38 criteria in WCAG 2.0. WCAG 2.1 added 12 at AA or below, making 50. WCAG 2.2 added 6 more and removed one, 4.1.1 Parsing, which asked for well-formed markup; W3C judged it obsolete because assistive technology no longer needs to parse HTML directly. A newer version is therefore not the older list plus extras: a contract for 2.1 AA still includes Parsing, and 2.2 AA does not.

Before choosing level A, predict whether Focus Visible is on its list. It is not. 2.4.7 has been level AA in 2.0, 2.1 and the final 2.2 text, although drafts of 2.2 moved it to level A from February 2020 until the May 2023 draft moved it back.

Opening a guideline shows where its count comes from: each criterion with its level and the version that added it. Guideline 2.4 Navigable has eight criteria at AA in 2.2, and one of them, 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), arrived in 2.2.

A level asks for its own criteria and those of the levels below it, in the WCAG version you name. The counts say what to evaluate; they do not test a page. Each criterion still has its own conditions and exceptions, conformance covers complete pages and processes, and a policy may name an older version than the one shown here.
