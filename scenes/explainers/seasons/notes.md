# Author notes

The model in `../lib/models.ts` computes day length, noon altitude, noon sunlight per square metre, the length of level ground a 1 m noon beam lands on (1 / sin of the altitude, drawn side by side for both solstices in the spread step) and orbital distance. The precise tilt is 23.44°. Declination uses a cosine approximation over a 365-day year; the distance formula is the one cited from Position of the Sun. The final chart compares each selected latitude with its mirror, and the bars compare noon sunlight with an overhead Sun at average distance.

Daylight times the Sun's centre above a flat horizon. It omits refraction, the Sun's disc and hills or buildings, so almanac days are longer and polar nights shorter. Noon sunlight omits atmospheric absorption. No weather or temperature is modelled. The four cities and their mirrored latitudes are the displayed cases; the tilt is fixed. The continuous yearly curve comes from the approximation, not an almanac validation of every date.
