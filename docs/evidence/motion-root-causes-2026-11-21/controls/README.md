## Nested ownership and clipping

Two independent groups display red words on the left and right. Between frames 2 and 40, independent moving masks temporarily remove both words. The old reporting code discarded the nested right owner's warning even though the native raster lost both words. The repaired checker identifies both owners with distinct or repeated wording. Clipping the right mask leaves the right word visible and produces only the left warning. [Native counts and findings](core-review.json)

A second scene admits two copies of “SAME” through a keyed transform. A narrow viewport on the right parent clips out its letters. The old transform exposed 245 right-hand red pixels at frame 52 and 284 at frame 60. The repaired transform keeps the right word clipped throughout, while the left control stays visible. Frame 68 shows the restored target hierarchy. The sheet contains native 480-pixel-wide strips from the full 480 × 270 stage. [Before and after frames](matched-clip-frames.png)

The lasting tests compare independently sought frames and exercise two moving masks, repeated words, three intersecting viewports, disjoint and zero-height clips, keyed and unkeyed ancestors, glyphs and backing paths. Reinstating each old behavior separately makes its corresponding assertion fail. ## Machine travel contracts

Twelve independent scenes exercise constructed and unplayed moves, the same key in two views, grouped and repeated replay, a masked ring and its inverse, and visible travel. The initial checker accepted a traveller hidden behind an opaque foreground throughout all 70 move frames and a required green caption that disappeared for nine frames per replay. The ring at the tested location contributed zero purple pixels through a clip over its hollow centre, compared with 942 pixels on every move frame with the clip removed. [Initial twelve scenarios](machine-rules-before.json) · [Initial native frame counts](machine-rules-before.frames.json.gz)

The repaired checker rejected the fully covered traveller and the missing caption on each replay. All 579 independently recorded move-frame observations matched across the twelve scenarios. A fully clipped ring, simultaneous trips of the same name in different views and a valid visible transfer remained accepted. [Final twelve scenarios](machine-rules-after.json) · [Final native frame counts](machine-rules-after.frames.json.gz)

The original aperture probe's strict red threshold missed a pink edge at frame 21. Counting every red contribution reveals four pixels at x = 396 in that frame, then sixteen at x = 399–402 in frame 22. This is a valid two-position witness. A narrower aperture exposes just one position per replay including all antialiasing, with twelve coloured pixels on frame 22 and none on the other 34 move frames. The original checker accepted the single-position flash. The repaired checker rejects both replays. The transfer control measures the source before the event, the traveller in the middle of the stage and the received object after landing. Both cases start with 560 painted green source pixels and finish with 560 destination pixels. Carrying the source clears every green pixel there during the crossing. Deliberately leaving the source behind retains all 560 and triggers its warning. A destination key names an entering representation, so an older representation there needs to leave before arrival. A source in a clipped container can intentionally release its full travel drawing. The source-survival assertion uses a source that the reader can see before departure.
