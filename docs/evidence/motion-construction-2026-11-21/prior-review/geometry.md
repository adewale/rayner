## Findings introduced against both baselines

### P1. A declared trip passes its movement contract with 31 identical rendered frames

**Normal trigger.** A wide parcel traverses four stage units behind a fixed rectangular viewport. A small patch under the parcel shares its color and moves entirely within the covered region. Across 31 samples of the begun public `Machine.send('ship')` event, the parcel's world x changes from −1.976 to 1.952. The complete 800 × 450 image has exactly one RGBA digest. With a static underlying patch, the machine checker rejects the trip. Moving only the hidden patch produces an empty finding array. A separate two-frame fixture passes the chapter route helper's assertion with byte-for-byte identical final images. [Public machine reproduction](geometry/machine-aperture.mjs) · [Machine receipt](geometry/machine-aperture.json) · [Chapter helper reproduction](geometry/probes.mjs)

**Cause and remedy.** The purported screen position is the weighted centroid of the difference between the actual frame and a render without the parcel. An invisible change to the background moves that centroid. The fact that the parcel's world geometry moved supplies the other half of the predicate. Require a corresponding change in the actual composited pixels at the old/new support of that specific traveller. In the native keeper, a fixed diagnostic tint or matte comparison can separate the traveller's visible support from colors behind it. Keep the existing knockout for detecting that some ink contributed. Prefer a conservative “movement unproved” when another moving layer makes attribution ambiguous. [Production centroid](../../tools/lib/explain-motion-rules.ts#L85) · [Production acceptance](../../tools/lib/explain-motion-rules.ts#L159) · [Chapter acceptance](../../scenes/explainers/lib/route-ink/fidelity.ts#L100)

### P2. Two briefly hidden labels become one continuous finding

Two unkeyed “OK” labels belong to one keyed panel and sit six units apart. Alternate two opaque covers between them every two frames. The native renderer shows each individual label gone for at most two frames, under the 0.1 s threshold even counting full display frames. One complete label is readable throughout. Head reports a single 0.367 s continuous loss and names all six cover spans. Holding the cover over the same physical label gives a genuine 0.367 s finding. Neither baseline contained this rule.

The per-frame record is keyed only by owner path and text. The first covered copy found in each frame extends the same run, even when it is the other label across the screen. Maintain continuity for the same physical text occurrence, using the leaf identity while retained and a position/glyph match for rebuilt seek frames. When two candidates cannot be distinguished, end the interval. This preserves the valid alternating-mask case without stitching different victims. [Grouping](../../src/keyed-motion.ts#L825) · [Run continuation](../../src/keyed-motion.ts#L835) · [Native results under `alternating-victims`](geometry/head-results.json)

### P2. One visible letter can suppress the loss of a complete word

An opaque plate covers “ABCD” for 15 frames. A co-located replacement has identical words and layout, with a real clip revealing only “A”. The original loses all 340 red glyph pixels. The foreground substitute contributes 92 green pixels compared with 409 for the complete control. The checker accepts both replacements. Omitting the replacement gives the expected 0.467 s finding.

A copy with one eligible glyph supplies `covered === 0`, and the full run boxes pass `samePlace`. The exemption never compares which glyphs survived. Require the substitute to supply the particular glyphs claimed lost at compatible positions. The existing sampled glyph boxes already provide an inexpensive positive control for ordinary handoffs. [Glyph sampling](../../src/keyed-motion.ts#L322) · [Handoff exemption](../../src/keyed-motion.ts#L835) · [Native results under `partial-handoff`](geometry/head-results.json)

### P2. Static geometry pays to rebuild curve data on every frame

On 120 static filled circles, zero labels and 61 frames, three fresh processes per revision produced median sampling times of 157 ms on head, 39 ms on main and 38 ms on the PR base. Median times through `finish()` were 172 ms, 48 ms and 48 ms respectively. Head retained about 128 MiB RSS after sampling, against 83–84 MiB in both baselines, from similar 73–75 MiB starting points. All nine runs returned zero findings. Elapsed measurements describe this bounded workload.

The sampler computes both anchor arrays and subdivided cubic hulls for every filled path and retains them per frame, even when no text is present. Delay curve preparation until there is a candidate label/mask pair. Where snapshots must survive subsequent animation, cache immutable point geometry by the existing point version, array identity and projection. This reduces duplicate work without weakening the current positive curve/clip controls. ## What I would keep

The conservative cubic hull/winding test, actual fill alpha and effective mask clip are valid progress. The pinned async Resvg change keeps the existing Promise API and has a relevant 320-image byte-parity experiment. The synchronous chapter helper remains separate from the production async wrapper. I found no new defect in that wrapper. The documented scope already excludes mask unions, partially clipped glyphs and unsupported CSS paints from the conservative stationary occlusion proof. [Geometry investigation](../../docs/evidence/motion-sol61-2026-11-21/geometry-framework.md) · [Raster experiment](../../docs/evidence/motion-raster-resource-2026-11-21/README.md)

The text rules can share a decision about which glyphs of a particular occurrence contribute visible ink. Use the existing clip/opacity/curve proof for cheap decisions and candidate bounds to limit work. Use a small native confirmation or abstain on ambiguous cases. Track duration on the occurrence that lost those glyphs. The scene-level pixel controls remain useful as independent witnesses of what the reader saw.
