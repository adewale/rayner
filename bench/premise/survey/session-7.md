4. **A ring buffer keeps a fixed-size array and moves two indexes, head and tail, around it instead of moving the data.** In most engines `array.shift()` is O(n), because every remaining element slides down one slot. A ring buffer avoids that. Pushing writes at `tail` and sets `tail = (tail + 1) % capacity`. Popping reads at `head` and advances `head` the same way. When an index passes the end, it wraps back to slot 0 and reuses space freed by earlier pops, so both operations are O(1) and nothing is reallocated. A count, or one deliberately empty slot, tells "full" apart from "empty". When it's full, you either reject the push, overwrite the oldest item, or grow the array. I'm confident about this.
