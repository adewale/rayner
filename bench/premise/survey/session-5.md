1. Express should call the client the first address, counting from the right of X-Forwarded-For, that isn't one of your own proxies. Each proxy appends the address it received the connection from to the end of the header. The left end is whatever the client sent, and the client can write anything there. If you set `trust proxy` to `true`, Express trusts every hop and `req.ip` becomes that leftmost, forgeable value. With a CDN in front of a load balancer, set it to `2` (the number of hops) or, better, to the CDN's and load balancer's IP ranges. Express then walks in from the right, skips the addresses it trusts, and uses the next one as `req.ip`. I'm confident about this.

2. Responses don't have to come back in the order the requests went out. Suppose the request for "ca" is slow and the one for "cat" is fast. "cat" resolves first and shows its results. Then "ca" resolves and its `.then` overwrites them with stale results. A promise only tells you a response arrived, not that it's still the one you want. To fix it, give each request an ID and ignore any response that isn't the latest. You can also cancel earlier requests with `AbortController`, or use `switchMap`-style logic. I'm confident about this.

4. Seasons come from Earth's tilt, not its distance from the Sun. Earth's axis is tilted about 23.4°. In July the northern hemisphere leans toward the Sun, so sunlight hits it more directly and days are longer. That outweighs being farthest away, at aphelion in early July. The distance only changes by about 3%, which makes about 7% difference in sunlight, too little to drive the seasons. The southern hemisphere has winter at the same moment, which a distance cause couldn't explain. I'm confident about this.

6. Renew when a set fraction of the lifetime has passed, not a fixed number of days before expiry. The common guidance is to renew with about a third of the lifetime left, or to follow the CA's ACME Renewal Information (ARI) window if it publishes one. A setting written for 90-day certificates, like Certbot's default of renewing 30 days before expiry, breaks as lifetimes shrink. With a 45-day certificate it renews after about 15 days. With a 6-day certificate it wants to renew the moment the certificate is issued, every time the job runs. Also check that your job runs often enough: twice a day is fine for very short certificates, but weekly isn't. Recent Certbot versions scale the threshold for short certificates, but I'm only moderately sure of the exact numbers.
